Here is the text by section. 



HERO SECTION:

A global movement bringing together people of every nation, culture, and background in the pursuit of moral responsibility, human dignity, spiritual understanding, and a shared future.

Inspired by the ancient vision of the nations gathering in Jerusalem, The Seventy Nations creates spaces where dialogue replaces division, wisdom guides leadership, and cooperation becomes the foundation for lasting peace.

Humanity has never possessed greater technological power, yet our greatest challenges remain deeply human. We believe the future will not be shaped by innovation alone, but by the moral courage, compassion, and shared responsibility of people working together across cultures and traditions.



2ND SECTION:

Why The Seventy Nations?

Humanity has never been more connected, yet division, conflict, isolation, and misunderstanding continue to separate people from one another.

The Seventy Nations exists to cultivate moral responsibility, strengthen human dignity, and create spaces where people can learn from one another with humility, curiosity, and respect.

We believe that lasting peace begins not only through institutions and governments, but through individuals who choose wisdom over fear, dialogue over division, and service over self-interest.

The future requires more than agreement.

It requires understanding.


Why Jerusalem?

For thousands of years Jerusalem has stood at the crossroads of civilization—where faith, history, culture, and humanity’s deepest questions have converged.

It is a city that has inspired reflection on justice, purpose, identity, and our relationship to one another.

The Seventy Nations envisions Jerusalem as a gathering place where people from every nation can learn together, celebrate our diversity, and contribute to a future grounded in wisdom, moral responsibility, and our shared humanity.


The Visionaries Behind The Seventy Nations

Great institutions are built through shared vision.

The Seventy Nations brings together leaders from diverse cultures, professions, and traditions who share a common commitment to strengthening human dignity, cultivating moral leadership, and building bridges between people, cultures, and nations.

Together we seek to create an institution that will serve generations to come.


3RD SECTION:

Founding Leadership

The founding leadership represents a growing international collaboration dedicated to creating a permanent institution where education, dialogue, cultural understanding, and spiritual exploration contribute to the flourishing of humanity.

Each founder brings unique experience, wisdom, and perspective while sharing one common vision:

A future where humanity learns to thrive together.


Rabbi Meir Yaaqov Ha-Levi

Rabbi Meir Yaaqov Ha-Levi is a Levite, educator, entrepreneur, and international advocate for ethical leadership. A graduate of the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology in Industrial Engineering and Management, he has founded and led international businesses and humanitarian initiatives while working with government and civic leaders around the world.

Today, Rabbi Ha-Levi serves as spokesman for the Sanhedrin in Jerusalem. His work focuses on promoting moral responsibility and advancing the Universal Ethical Common Code (UECC)—a practical framework that encourages integrity, accountability, and cooperation among individuals, businesses, and nations. He and his wife are blessed with eleven children and twenty grandchildren.


DW Duke

DW Duke is an accomplished trial attorney, author, and lecturer with decades of experience in law, leadership, and human rights. He is the author of six books, including The Duke Legacy and Not Without a Fight, and has written extensively on legal, ethical, and public policy issues.

He earned his Bachelor of Arts from the University of Michigan and his Juris Doctor from Washington University School of Law. Mr. Duke serves as President of the Institute for Children’s Aid and brings strategic legal and organizational leadership to the Foundation.

Rabbi Yeshayahu HaKohen Hollander (1937–2024)

Rabbi Yeshayahu HaKohen Hollander was a respected Torah scholar, educator, and pioneer in Jewish-Noahide and interfaith relations. Born in New York, he immigrated to Israel in 1948 and studied at Yeshiva Ponevezh and Kollel Volozhin under Jerusalem Chief Rabbi Yitzhak Kolitz.

He served as Foreign Minister of the re-established Sanhedrin, helped develop the Easy-to-Read Talmud project, and devoted many years to building relationships between Israel and the nations through education, dialogue, and shared ethical values.

Meirah M’roz (Marianne Paradise)

Meirah M’roz is the founder of The Seventy Nations Jerusalem and visionary behind the Jerusalem Unity Center—an international initiative dedicated to cultivating ethical leadership, spiritual understanding, and cooperation among the nations through the wisdom of Jerusalem.

A musician, author, and longtime advocate for peace and interreligious dialogue, she received an Apostolic Blessing from Pope John Paul II in recognition of her efforts to promote unity centered on the understanding of the One God. After immigrating to Israel in 2006 and receiving the Israeli New Immigrant Artist Award, she dedicated her work to building bridges between faiths while deepening appreciation for the Torah’s universal moral teachings.

Her vision is to establish Jerusalem as a global center where people of every nation can learn, collaborate, and grow together in service to God and humanity.


Co-Founder Legacy

Rabbi Yeshayahu HaKohen Hollander co-founded The Seventy Nations Jerusalem Foundation with Meirah M’roz to help strengthen relationships between Israel and the nations through the universal ethical teachings of the Torah. Together they established the vision for the Jerusalem Unity Center—a future House of Learning that welcomes people of every nation to discover the God of Israel, deepen moral responsibility, and participate in the prophetic vision of Jerusalem as a House of Prayer for All Nations.

5TH SECTION

Designed as a Center of Consciousness, Spiritual Intelligence, and Human Flourishing, it will welcome people from every nation to explore moral development, dialogue, leadership, culture, scientific inquiry, creativity, and the shared search for wisdom.

The physical campus and digital campus will function as one integrated institution—creating opportunities for learning, collaboration, research, and community participation across the world.

Join us as we build a global movement dedicated to human flourishing, international cooperation, and the enduring hope that wisdom can unite what division has separated.
